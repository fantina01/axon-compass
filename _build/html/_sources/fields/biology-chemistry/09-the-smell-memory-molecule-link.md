*Basic Chemistry of Scent and the Brain's Memory Center*


```{admonition} Why This Matters
:class: note

```

## Core Literature — Read This Before Starting Unit II

Real, verified sources for this project. Read these before Unit II begins — they're what your literature review and hypothesis should be built on.

### 1. Herz, R. S., & Engen, T. (1996). Odor memory: Review and analysis. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 3(3), 300-313.

Herz and Engen's review compiles evidence that odor-triggered memories tend to be unusually vivid and emotionally powerful compared to memories triggered by other senses, and discusses the underlying anatomical explanation: the smell-processing pathway connects more directly to memory- and emotion-related brain areas than other senses do, which typically route through more intermediate processing stations first. This is the foundational review behind the project's central claim and its proposed anatomical explanation.

### 2. Herz, R. S. (2004). A naturalistic analysis of autobiographical memories triggered by olfactory visual and auditory stimuli. Chemical Senses, 29(3), 217-224.

In this study, Herz directly compared memories triggered by smell versus sight or sound for the same real events, finding that odor-triggered memories were rated as significantly more emotional and evocative than memories of the same events triggered by other senses. This is a direct, controlled comparison supporting the project's specific experimental design, which similarly compares recall performance across different sensory conditions.

### 3. Soudry, Y., Lemogne, C., Malinvaud, D., Consoli, S. M., & Bonfils, P. (2011). Olfactory system and emotion: Common substrates. European Annals of Otorhinolaryngology, Head and Neck Diseases, 128(1), 18-23.

This review details the specific anatomical pathway from the olfactory bulb to the amygdala and hippocampus (key emotion and memory structures), explaining structurally why smell has a more direct route to these areas than vision or hearing, which pass through the thalamus first. This is the specific anatomical evidence the project's diagram should be built on, going beyond the general claim into the actual brain pathway involved.

**Steps specific to this project:**

- [ ] **Hypothesis:** peers who learn the information paired with the specific scent will show better recall when re-exposed to that scent during testing than peers with no scent or a mismatched scent during testing.
