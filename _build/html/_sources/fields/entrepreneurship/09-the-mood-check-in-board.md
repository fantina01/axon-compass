## Core Literature — Read This Before Starting Unit II

Real, verified sources for this project. Read these before Unit II begins — they're what your literature review and hypothesis should be built on.

### 1. Lieberman, M. D., Eisenberger, N. I., Crockett, M. J., Tom, S. M., Pfeifer, J. H., & Way, B. M. (2007). Putting feelings into words: Affect labeling disrupts amygdala activity in response to affective stimuli. Psychological Science, 18(5), 421-428.

Using brain imaging, Lieberman and colleagues found that simply labeling an emotional experience with a word (like naming a feeling as "angry") measurably reduced activity in the amygdala, a brain region central to emotional reactivity, compared to processing the same emotional stimulus without labeling it. This is the direct neuroscience evidence behind the project's core premise: the simple act of naming and tracking a mood, even briefly, has a real, measurable calming effect on the brain.

### 2. Kircanski, K., Lieberman, M. D., & Craske, M. G. (2012). Feelings into words: Contributions of language to exposure therapy. Psychological Science, 23(10), 1086-1091.

This study found that participants who labeled their emotions while confronting a feared stimulus (in exposure therapy for a phobia) showed better outcomes than those using other coping strategies, extending the affect-labeling finding into a more applied, real-world therapeutic context. This adds practical, applied evidence for why a simple, regular mood-naming practice — like the project's check-in board — could have real benefit beyond the laboratory.

### 3. Torre, J. B., & Lieberman, M. D. (2018). Putting feelings into words: Affect labeling as implicit emotion regulation. Emotion Review, 10(2), 116-124.

This review updates and synthesizes the affect-labeling research a decade after the original 2007 study, confirming the core finding has replicated reasonably well while adding nuance about exactly when and how labeling helps most. It's a useful, current source for grounding the project's premise in the most up-to-date understanding of the research, not just the original single study.

## Skills This Project Builds

- Revising a product design based on real, sometimes disappointing usage data.

## Why This Project Impresses

Real adoption data — including honest results if it didn't work perfectly — is exactly the kind of real-world, unpolished proof mentors and accelerators respect far more than a hypothetical success story.

## How the Six Outputs Link Into One Portfolio

Every unit of this project is the same simple, real tool, tested honestly for the hardest thing any product faces: whether people actually keep using it. Unit I sets up the real board in a real classroom. Unit II tracks real, honest daily usage over two full weeks, not just a one-time trial. Unit III shares the honest full story, including any drop-off in usage, not just a polished success story. Unit IV shows the real usage chart. Unit V is a real, possibly revised pitch based on what actually happened. A mentor sees something rarer and more valuable than a success story: honest, real adoption data, including the parts that didn't go perfectly.
