## Core Literature — Read This Before Starting Unit II

Real, verified sources for this project. Read these before Unit II begins — they're what your literature review and hypothesis should be built on.

### 1. Jerath, R., Edry, J. W., Barnes, V. A., & Jerath, V. (2006). Physiology of long pulmonary breath yoga: Neural respiratory elements may provide a mechanism that explains how slow deep breathing shifts the autonomic nervous system. Medical Hypotheses, 67(3), 566-571.

Jerath and colleagues propose a physiological mechanism explaining why slow, deep breathing shifts the body from a stress-activated state toward a calmer one — specifically, slow exhalation appears to stimulate the vagus nerve, which helps activate the body's calming (parasympathetic) response. This is the mechanistic basis for the project's entire premise: a specific breathing pattern, not breathing in general, is what produces the calming effect.

### 2. Zaccaro, A., Piarulli, A., Laurino, M., et al. (2018). How breath-control can change your life: A systematic review on psycho-physiological correlates of slow breathing. Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, 12, 353.

This systematic review compiled evidence across many studies confirming that slow-paced breathing (generally under about 10 breaths per minute) is reliably associated with reduced physiological stress markers and improved self-reported calm, and discusses the vagal and autonomic mechanisms involved. This gives the project's specific two-minute breathing protocol a real evidence base for its exact pacing choice, not just a general "breathing helps" claim.

### 3. Arch, J. J., & Craske, M. G. (2006). Mechanisms of mindfulness: Emotion regulation following a focused breathing induction. Behaviour Research and Therapy, 44(12), 1849-1858.

This controlled study specifically tested a brief focused-breathing exercise (similar in length to the project's two-minute tool) and found it produced measurable reductions in negative emotional response to a subsequent stressor, compared to a control condition. This is directly useful because it's a short-duration intervention closely matching the project's own time frame, rather than a longer meditation practice.
