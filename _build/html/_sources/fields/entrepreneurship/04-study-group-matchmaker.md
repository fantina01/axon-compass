## Core Literature — Read This Before Starting Unit II

Real, verified sources for this project. Read these before Unit II begins — they're what your literature review and hypothesis should be built on.

### 1. Roediger, H. L., & Karpicke, J. D. (2006). Test-enhanced learning: Taking memory tests improves long-term retention. Psychological Science, 17(3), 249-255.

(Verified in the Education field's "Testing as Teaching" project.) The core testing-effect finding — that actively retrieving information strengthens memory more than passive review — underlies part of why study groups can outperform solo studying: explaining material to a study partner is itself a form of active retrieval practice.

### 2. Chase, C. C., Chin, D. B., Oppezzo, M. A., & Schwartz, D. L. (2009). Teachable agents and the protégé effect: Increasing the effort towards learning. Journal of Science Education and Technology, 18(4), 334-352.

This research demonstrates the "protégé effect": students who prepare to teach material to someone else (even a simulated learner) study harder and retain more than students preparing only for their own test, because responsibility for someone else's understanding increases motivation and engagement with the material. This is the direct evidence behind the project's core claim that a matched study group — where members explain material to each other — should outperform solo studying.

### 3. Fiorella, L., & Mayer, R. E. (2013). The relative benefits of learning by teaching and teaching expectancy. Contemporary Educational Psychology, 38(4), 281-288.

This study specifically compared students who genuinely taught material to a peer against students who only expected to teach but didn't, finding actual teaching produced significantly better learning outcomes than merely preparing to teach. This is useful, precise evidence for the project's specific design: it's the real act of explaining to another group member, not just intending to, that should drive the benefit the matching service is built around.
