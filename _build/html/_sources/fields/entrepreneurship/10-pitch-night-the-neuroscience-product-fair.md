## Core Literature — Read This Before Starting Unit II

Real, verified sources for this project. Read these before Unit II begins — they're what your literature review and hypothesis should be built on.

### 1. Hattie, J., & Timperley, H. (2007). The power of feedback. Review of Educational Research, 77(1), 81-112.

This influential, widely-cited review analyzed what makes feedback actually useful for improving performance, finding that specific, structured feedback focused on the task and the process (not just praise or criticism of the person) is significantly more effective at driving real improvement. This is the direct evidence behind the project's central hypothesis: a structured feedback form should produce more useful feedback for presenters than open-ended "any thoughts?" questions.

### 2. Shute, V. J. (2008). Focus on formative feedback. Review of Educational Research, 78(1), 153-189.

Shute's review specifically examines "formative feedback" — feedback given during a process to guide improvement, rather than just a final evaluation — and identifies key features of effective feedback, including that it should be specific, actionable, and appropriately timed. This is directly useful for designing the project's own structured feedback form, since it identifies concrete features (not just "be specific") the form's questions should be built around.

### 3. Nicol, D. J., & Macfarlane-Dick, D. (2006). Formative assessment and self-regulated learning: A model and seven principles of good feedback practice. Studies in Higher Education, 31(2), 199-218.

This paper lays out seven evidence-based principles for effective feedback, including clarifying what good performance looks like and delivering feedback that helps the recipient close the gap between current and desired performance. This is a useful, more detailed practical framework the project's structured feedback form can be explicitly built around and evaluated against.

### Unit II — Research Investigation
*Weeks 5–8 · Required artifact: **Research Investigation / Prospectus***

**Process for this unit (same for every project in the chapter):** Week 6 **Prediction Ladder** tournament + Week 7 **Build-a-System** laboratory.

**Steps specific to this project:**

- [ ] **Literature review:** not neuroscience-of-the-brain this time, but plain-language best practices for running a fair, useful feedback event (short pitches, structured feedback forms).
- [ ] **Hypothesis:** structured feedback forms will produce more useful, specific feedback for presenters than open-ended "any thoughts?" questions.
- [ ] **Variables:** feedback format (structured form vs. open-ended) as the thing compared; how specific/useful presenters rate the feedback they received as the outcome.
- [ ] **Methodology:** test both feedback formats with different judges or different pitches at the same event, and compare presenter ratings of usefulness.
