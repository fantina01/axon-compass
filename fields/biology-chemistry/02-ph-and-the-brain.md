# 2. pH and the Brain

*How Blood Chemistry Balance Affects Mental Clarity*

**Field:** Neuroscience × Biology & Chemistry &nbsp;·&nbsp; **Project ID:** `biology-chemistry/02-ph-and-the-brain`

```{admonition} Big Question
:class: tip
Can basic pH chemistry (something students already learn with litmus paper and acid/base reactions) explain why things like dehydration or poor breathing habits affect mental focus?
```

```{admonition} Why This Matters
:class: note
The brain is extremely sensitive to small changes in blood chemistry, including pH balance, and connecting this directly to standard pH lessons (already taught with simple test strips) gives a concrete, testable link between basic chemistry class and everyday mental performance.
```

## Core Literature — Read This Before Starting Unit II

Real, verified sources for this project. Read these before Unit II begins — they're what your literature review and hypothesis should be built on.

### 1. Adan, A. (2012). Cognitive performance and dehydration. Journal of the American College of Nutrition, 31(2), 71-78.

Adan's review compiled evidence that even mild dehydration (as little as 1-2% of body weight in fluid loss) is associated with measurable declines in attention, short-term memory, and mood, particularly in tasks requiring sustained concentration. This is the direct evidence behind the project's central hypothesis that hydration level affects classroom attention performance.

### 2. Ganio, M. S., Armstrong, L. E., Casa, D. J., et al. (2011). Mild dehydration impairs cognitive performance and mood of men. British Journal of Nutrition, 106(10), 1535-1543.

This controlled study specifically measured cognitive performance in men under mild dehydration versus properly hydrated conditions, finding measurable declines in tasks requiring vigilance and working memory under dehydration, alongside increased self-reported fatigue and difficulty concentrating. This adds a controlled, experimental (not just observational) source supporting the project's hypothesis, with real measured effect sizes.

### 3. Boron, W. F., & Boulpaep, E. L. (2016). Medical Physiology (3rd ed.). Elsevier. (Chapter on acid-base physiology).

This standard medical physiology textbook explains how the body's buffering systems (including breathing rate and kidney function) work together to maintain a stable blood pH, and how relatively small disruptions to this balance can affect neural function, since neurons are highly sensitive to their surrounding chemical environment. This is the foundational acid-base physiology reference underlying the project's broader claim that blood chemistry balance affects the brain, connecting standard chemistry-class pH concepts to real physiology.

### Unit II — Research Investigation
*Weeks 5–8 · Required artifact: **Research Investigation / Prospectus***

**Process for this unit (same for every project in the chapter):** Week 6 **Prediction Ladder** tournament + Week 7 **Build-a-System** laboratory.

**Steps specific to this project:**

- [ ] **Literature review:** basic explanations of how the body maintains blood pH balance, and published research linking mild dehydration to reduced attention and mood.
- [ ] **Hypothesis:** peers who are more hydrated (based on self-tracked water intake) will score higher on a short attention test than when they are less hydrated, measured within the same individuals on different days.
- [ ] **Variables:** hydration level as the thing being compared; attention-test score as the outcome.
- [ ] **Methodology:** within-person comparison across multiple days with varying reported water intake, using a simple, repeatable attention test (like a letter-cancellation task).

## How the Six Outputs Link Into One Portfolio

Every artifact here answers one question with more precision: does the water fountain actually matter for your grade? Unit I's tracker collects the first real hydration-and-focus data. Unit II turns it into a genuine within-person comparison across real school days. Unit III explains that finding to students who've never connected chemistry-class pH lessons to their own afternoon focus. Unit IV visualizes the buffering system the Unit II data is testing. Unit V turns the whole thing into a real, low-cost water-access policy ask. A science or health mentor sees standard acid-base chemistry applied to something a student can test on themselves and then hand to their own principal.
