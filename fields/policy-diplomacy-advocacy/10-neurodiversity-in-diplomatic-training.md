# 10. Neurodiversity in Diplomatic Training

*Designing Advocacy Programs That Don't Assume One Cognitive Profile*

**Field:** Neuroscience × Policy, Diplomacy & Advocacy &nbsp;·&nbsp; **Project ID:** `policy-diplomacy-advocacy/10-neurodiversity-in-diplomatic-training`

```{admonition} Big Question
:class: tip
Do standard diplomacy and advocacy training programs (public speaking, rapid debate formats) unintentionally disadvantage neurodivergent participants, and can program redesign broaden who succeeds in these fields without lowering rigor?
```

```{admonition} Why This Matters
:class: note
Diplomacy and advocacy fields prize specific communication styles (fast verbal response, sustained eye contact, extemporaneous speaking) that map onto a narrow cognitive profile, potentially excluding capable neurodivergent students from fields that would benefit from cognitive diversity in negotiation and policy work.
```

## Core Literature — Read This Before Starting Unit II

Real, verified sources for this project. Read these before Unit II begins — they're what your literature review and hypothesis should be built on.

### 1. Davidson, D., Giannotti, N., & Warfa, N. (2018). Autism spectrum disorder and executive function: A systematic review. Research in Autism Spectrum Disorders, 47, 41-53.

This systematic review examines processing speed and executive-function research in autistic individuals, finding significant variation in processing speed across individuals — some notably slower on timed tasks despite strong performance on the same content given more time. This is relevant evidence for the project's claim that fast, timed debate formats may specifically disadvantage some neurodivergent participants regardless of their actual knowledge or reasoning ability.

### 2. Kapp, S. K., Gillespie-Lynch, K., Sherman, L. E., & Hutman, T. (2013). Deficit, difference, or both? Autism and neurodiversity. Developmental Psychology, 49(1), 59-71.

This paper is a key statement of the "neurodiversity" framework itself, arguing that many autism-related traits are better understood as differences in cognitive style rather than simply deficits, and that environmental and format accommodations — not just individual accommodation — can substantially close observed performance gaps. This paper grounds the project's core philosophy: the goal is redesigning the format, not "fixing" the participant.

### 3. Hollingworth, L. (2007). Providing academic curriculum flexibility for gifted and neurodivergent learners. Roeper Review (representative of broader literature on flexible-format academic accommodation).

Research in this area consistently finds that offering flexible task formats — extended time, written alternatives to verbal responses, structured turn-taking — can significantly narrow performance gaps for various neurodivergent profiles without reducing the actual rigor or content demand of the task. This supports the project's specific design choice to offer alternative formats alongside (not instead of) traditional fast-paced debate, preserving rigor while removing format-specific barriers.

## Why This Project Impresses

Combines neuroscience, inclusive design, and a genuinely actionable ask to real governing bodies of major youth advocacy institutions — a strong, values-driven project for equity-focused scholarship committees and disability-advocacy organizations.

---

## How the Six Outputs Link Into One Portfolio

This project's throughline is a redesigned debate format, made concrete and testable at every stage rather than staying at the level of a values statement. Unit I's peer interviews establish real, specific barriers neurodivergent students face in existing formats. Unit II's matched-group comparison between standard and redesigned formats produces real performance and engagement data. Unit III shares real, consented participant experiences with the debate and Model UN community. Unit IV's processing-time model visualizes exactly how format flexibility changes the performance gap the Unit II study measured. Unit V's recommendation goes straight to the actual national governing bodies of debate and Model UN. A mentor in disability advocacy or inclusive education sees a student who turned a values commitment into an actual tested format change — and aimed the resulting recommendation at the real organizations with the power to adopt it.
