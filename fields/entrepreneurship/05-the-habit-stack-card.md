## Core Literature — Read This Before Starting Unit II

Real, verified sources for this project. Read these before Unit II begins — they're what your literature review and hypothesis should be built on.

### 1. Wood, W., & Rünger, D. (2016). Psychology of habit. Annual Review of Psychology, 67, 289-314.

(Verified in the Computer Science field's "Habit Loop Tracker" project.) Wood and Rünger's review of how repeated behavior in a consistent context becomes automatic is the direct theoretical basis for "habit stacking" — attaching a new habit to an existing, already-automatic one provides a reliable, consistent cue.

### 2. Gardner, B., Lally, P., & Wardle, J. (2012). Making health habitual: The psychology of 'habit-formation' and general practice. British Journal of General Practice, 62(605), 664-666.

This practical paper specifically discusses "habit stacking" — attaching a new desired behavior to an existing, well-established routine — as an evidence-informed strategy for building new habits more reliably than relying on willpower or motivation alone, since it borrows the existing routine's strong, automatic cue. This is the direct, applied source behind the project's specific card design.

### 3. Lally, P., van Jaarsveld, C. H. M., Potts, H. W. W., & Wardle, J. (2010). How are habits formed: Modelling habit formation in the real world. European Journal of Social Psychology, 40(6), 998-1009.

(Verified in the Computer Science field's "Habit Loop Tracker" project.) This real-world tracking study found habit strength increases with repetition but that occasional missed days don't seriously disrupt the process — useful, realistic evidence for how the project's card should frame setbacks to real users trying to build a new habit.

*Weeks 5–8 · Required artifact: **Research Investigation / Prospectus***

## How the Six Outputs Link Into One Portfolio

Every unit of this project is the same physical, sellable product, tested more rigorously at each stage. Unit I designs and makes the first real batch. Unit II tests it against a real control group building the same habit without it. Unit III shares those real results with potential future buyers or distributors. Unit IV shows the honest streak-length data for every real participant. Unit V is a real pitch to the school store or a fundraiser to actually distribute it. A mentor sees a physically real, testable, sellable product with real usage data — about as concrete as entrepreneurial proof gets.
