## Core Literature — Read This Before Starting Unit II

Real, verified sources for this project. Read these before Unit II begins — they're what your literature review and hypothesis should be built on.

### 1. Benton, D. (2002). Carbohydrate ingestion, blood glucose and mood. Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews, 26(3), 293-308.

(Verified in the Biology & Chemistry field's "Fermentation and Focus" project.) Benton's review found that snacks producing a gradual, sustained blood glucose response are generally associated with better sustained attention than high-sugar snacks producing a rapid spike and subsequent drop — the direct evidence behind the project's snack-selection rationale.

### 2. Cooper, S. B., Bandelow, S., & Nevill, M. E. (2011). Breakfast consumption and cognitive function in adolescent schoolchildren. Nutrition Research Reviews, 24(2), 148-160.

(Verified in the Biology & Chemistry field's "Fermentation and Focus" project.) This review focuses specifically on adolescent students and found meal glycemic quality affects classroom-relevant cognitive measures — directly relevant since the project's snack cart serves the same age group in the same real school context.

### 3. Micha, R., Rogers, P. J., & Nelson, M. (2011). Glycaemic index and glycaemic load of breakfast predict cognitive function and mood in school children: A randomised controlled trial. British Journal of Nutrition, 106(10), 1552-1561.

This randomized controlled trial specifically compared low- versus high-glycemic-index foods in a school setting and measured effects on children's cognitive performance and mood over the following hours, finding real, measurable differences favoring lower-glycemic options. This is directly relevant, high-quality experimental evidence (a randomized trial, not just an observational study) supporting the project's core hypothesis in a school-food context.

### Unit II — Research Investigation
*Weeks 5–8 · Required artifact: **Research Investigation / Prospectus***

**Process for this unit (same for every project in the chapter):** Week 6 **Prediction Ladder** tournament + Week 7 **Build-a-System** laboratory.

**Steps specific to this project:**

- [ ] **Literature review:** basic science of blood sugar and focus, explained simply as the reason for the snack choices.
- [ ] **Hypothesis:** customers who buy the focus-friendly snacks will report feeling more alert an hour later than they typically do after a sugary snack.
- [ ] **Variables:** snack type as the thing compared; self-rated alertness an hour later as the outcome.
- [ ] **Methodology:** ask real customers to rate their alertness afterward, and compare to their own typical experience with sugary snacks.
