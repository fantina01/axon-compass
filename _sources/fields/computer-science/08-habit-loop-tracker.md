## Core Literature — Read This Before Starting Unit II

Real, verified sources for this project. Read these before Unit II begins — they're what your literature review and hypothesis should be built on.

### 1. Wood, W., & Rünger, D. (2016). Psychology of habit. Annual Review of Psychology, 67, 289-314.

Wood and Rünger's comprehensive review explains the modern scientific understanding of habit formation — how repeated behavior in a consistent context becomes increasingly automatic and cue-triggered over time, requiring less conscious decision-making with each repetition. This is the foundational review behind the project's cue-routine-reward design, updating older, more popular accounts of habit formation with current research.

### 2. Lally, P., van Jaarsveld, C. H. M., Potts, H. W. W., & Wardle, J. (2010). How are habits formed: Modelling habit formation in the real world. European Journal of Social Psychology, 40(6), 998-1009.

This real-world study tracked participants forming a new habit over several months and found habit strength (measured by how automatic the behavior felt) increased following a curve similar to the project's own streak-tracking concept, and that missing a single day occasionally did not seriously disrupt habit formation, contrary to popular "you have to start over" beliefs. This is directly useful, practical evidence for how the project's tracker should be designed and what it should communicate to users about occasional missed days.

### 3. Graybiel, A. M. (2008). Habits, rituals, and the evaluative brain. Annual Review of Neuroscience, 31, 359-387.

Graybiel's review describes the neural basis of habit formation, particularly the role of the basal ganglia in gradually taking over control of well-practiced behaviors from more effortful, prefrontal-cortex-driven decision-making. This is useful background connecting the project's behavioral cue-routine-reward model to the underlying brain systems actually responsible for habit automaticity.

**Steps specific to this project:**

- [ ] **Literature review:** plain-language summaries of habit-formation research (cue, routine, reward loop), plus basic programming concepts (variables that track a count, simple conditional messages).
- [ ] **Hypothesis:** peers using the tracker app for a simple habit (like a few minutes of daily reading) will maintain the habit for more consecutive days than peers trying the same habit without any tracking tool.

- [ ] **Methodology:** two peer groups attempt the same simple habit over a set period, one with the tracker and one without, and streak lengths are compared.
